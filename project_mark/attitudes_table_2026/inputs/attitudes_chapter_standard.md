# Statistical Compendium, attitudes chapter: table standard

Scenario document. Written by the task author to state the standard the compendium's attitudes chapter applies
to its table of social indicators in this task. It is not a NORC publication. The survey data it is applied to
is the General Social Survey cumulative data file, real and unaltered.

1. Source. The table is built from the General Social Survey cumulative data file as supplied in the
   workspace, with variables, codes, years and ballots as the codebook defines them.

2. Indicators. The table carries the indicators of the previous edition, each defined by its published wording
   and the response or responses it counts. An indicator is the share of respondents who gave a counted
   response among the respondents who gave any of the question's listed responses in that year. Respondents
   without a listed response, for whatever reason, are outside the denominator.

3. Years. The table shows the first edition year (the 1987 survey), the base year ten years before the edition
   (the 2014 survey) and the edition year (the 2024 survey). An indicator not fielded in a year carries no
   figure for that year. An indicator without an edition year figure carries no change and is marked as not
   updated.

4. Weight. Every figure in the edition is weighted by the person post stratification weight, WTSSPS. All years
   in the table are on the same weight; when the weight changes between editions, the figures of earlier years
   are restated on the new weight.

5. Continuity. Before an indicator is carried into a new edition, the figures the previous edition published
   for it are reproduced from the current file under the previous edition's method, as that edition's note
   records it, to the published precision. An indicator whose published figures do not reproduce is held out
   of the edition and listed as held.

6. Change. The decade change is the edition year figure less the base year figure, in percentage points. Its
   sampling error is estimated under the survey design, from the variance strata and primary sampling units
   carried in the file, with each survey year an independent sample.

7. Reporting. The edition reports an indicator as moved when its decade change is at least 1.96 times its
   sampling error, in either direction. The test is made on unrounded values.

8. Precision. Figures are published to one decimal place in percent, changes to one decimal place in points
   and sampling errors to two decimal places.
